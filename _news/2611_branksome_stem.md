On **19 November 2026**, I joined the **STEM Speaker Series at Branksome Hall STEM Council, Canada**, for an online conversation about my career journey. Thank you to **Sophia Zhu**, a student at the school, for inviting me to share my experiences with such a curious and thoughtful group of young women!

In my talk, **“My Journey as a Woman in GIS: From Industry to GeoAI Academia,”** I reflected on the path from high school through university, work in industry and a PhD, to my current role in GeoAI research and teaching at the University of Glasgow. We talked about why I chose this field, what I knew about it at school, and what a typical working day looks like. I also shared the parts of my work I enjoy most, challenges along the way, moments of growth, and advice for young women considering a career in GIS and STEM.

The Q&A brought some especially thoughtful questions. We discussed how **AI could shape higher education** and my everyday work in research and teaching, as well as how I sustain **motivation and passion during long commitments such as a PhD**.

One conversation took me back to my own university choices: **journalism was my first choice** when I ranked my options after the college entrance examination, yet my path led me to geography. It was lovely to reflect on how that unexpected direction has continued to inspire and motivate me over the years.

It was a deeply rewarding afternoon. I could feel the students’ curiosity in their questions, and I was so happy to hear that sharing my experiences had encouraged them. **Thank you, Sophia, the STEM Council and everyone who joined, for the warm welcome and engaging conversation — and for making this wonderfully creative poster!**
