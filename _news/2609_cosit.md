Fengjiao Li and I attended the [17th Conference on Spatial Information Theory (COSIT 2026)](https://www.cosit2026.uk/) in York, UK, from **22–25 September**. The conference theme, **Cognition and Flows**, brought together work on how people and systems experience, represent and reason about space.

Fengjiao presented our short paper, **“Spatiotemporal Adaptive Learning for Flow-Based Graph Neural Networks,”** by Fengjiao Li, Meiliu Wu and Ana Basiri. The paper is published open access in the COSIT 2026 proceedings, *Leibniz International Proceedings in Informatics* (LIPIcs, volume 393, pages 31:1–31:9). [Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.COSIT.2026.31).

Our study learns how much different spatial relationships—geographic adjacency, road connectivity and mobility links—matter when forecasting COVID-19 dynamics in Glasgow and Edinburgh. It also learns how to weight different forecasting horizons, making the model’s spatial and temporal choices explicit.

It was a pleasure to share the conference with Fengjiao and take part in thoughtful conversations across spatial information theory and GeoAI. **Thank you to the COSIT 2026 organising committee and session chairs** for a welcoming conference and an engaging programme.
